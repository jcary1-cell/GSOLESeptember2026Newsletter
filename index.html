President's Message

Dear GSOLE Members,

Let me begin by welcoming many of you to your new school years. I know some are a few weeks into your academic year and others are gearing up to start.

I wanted to take this opportunity to publicly reflect on a debate that arose during the most recent Cyber Salon. We were graced by the presence of the organization's founding leaders, Beth Hewett and Scott Warnock. Towards the end of the Salon, Beth-in light of the turmoil in writing studies-wrote in the Zoom chat that in hindsight she wishes that the organization had been founded on digitality rather than online. Her point is justified in the book Teaching Writing in the 21st Century that she co-authored with Warnock and Tiffany Bourelle. The authors correctly contend, "digital instruction is a natural part of [writing instructors'] twenty-first-century teaching environment and not a specialty area they can choose to address or ignore" (p. 4). This is an excellent point; digital media permeate our pedagogies whether we want it to or not, and writing instructors need to address the ways these digital technologies affect their and their students' practices. While digital technologies like GenAI have made some writing instructors want to return to 19th century composing practices, some institutions-like mine-require instructors to use the Learning Management Systems (LMSs) regardless of their teaching modality-face-to-face, hybrid, hy-flex, or completely online. And this does not account for the fact that most social and workplace communication in the 21st century is digitally mediated.

However, during the Salon, I respectfully disagreed. They are different pedagogies. Digital, I believe, emphasizes the technologies we use to teach, online points to the learning that happens when there is distance between ourselves and our students-ranging from the entire semester/quarter to the hours between Monday's and Wednesday's class. I understand digital writing pedagogies and online writing pedagogies overlap in many ways and that online writing instructors almost exclusively use digital technologies to mediate their courses. I also understand the omnipresence of LMSs means all writing instructors must leverage these digital platforms' teaching and learning affordances. While I think GSOLE members, along with our friends in the 7Cs, offer writing studies great resources and strategies to design digital pedagogies, I think GSOLE members can uniquely lead conversations about using these digital tools to pedagogically bridge the spatial gap between ourselves and our students.

I retell my account of the Cyber Salon not to get the last word and declare myself champion. Instead, I see the value of Beth's argument, and I have been mulling the issue for the last week. But I also believe that as an organization focused on online literacy education, we offer writing studies something unique. And, arguably, a lot of good teaching innovations begin with online pedagogies because of the MacGyver-esque approach we are often compelled to dream up just to create good teaching and learning. As I wrote last month, writing studies is at an inflection point, and GSOLE is also deciding how to position itself within the field. Members of GSOLE have a lot to contribute to how writing instructors of all teaching modalities adopt digital pedagogies, especially GenAI pedagogies-a topic that has weighed heavily on the field for the last four years. We also have a lot to contribute to discussions about online writing instruction. As writing studies organizes and defines itself, I encourage us to continue to have these discussions about the focus and scope of the Global Society of Online Literacy Education. I look forward to having these conversations with you.

Best,

Kevin DePew, GSOLE President

kdepew@odu.edu

GSOLE Webinar: Yoimar Falcon’s , Building Reading Bridges: Durable Reading Practices for Multilingual Learners in Online and Hybrid College Courses

Reading in online and hybrid learning environments often happens privately. Students may complete an assigned article, chapter, or digital text without instructors being able to see how they approached the reading, where comprehension became difficult, what connections they made, or how their thinking changed. For multilingual learners, the reading process can involve additional layers of linguistic, cultural, and academic meaning-making that may remain equally invisible.

This interactive webinar explores durable reading practices that can help instructors, tutors, and other educators make students’ reading processes more visible while supporting deeper engagement with texts. Rather than centering instruction on a particular platform or application, the session focuses on reading practices that can transfer across technologies, courses, languages, and learning environments. The emphasis remains on reading itself: how readers enter a text, monitor understanding, make connections, interpret evidence, and leave a visible trace of their thinking.

Participants Will Gain:

  • A transferable framework for making students’ reading processes visible in online and hybrid environments.
  • Practical strategies for supporting multilingual readers as they engage with complex academic texts.
  • Approaches for using brief writing and annotation to reveal reading comprehension and meaning-making.
  • Ideas for creating collaborative and accessible reading experiences without depending on one particular technology.
  • A practical plan for adapting one durable reading routine to a text, course, tutoring session, or learning environment they already use.

New Position: GSOLE Archivist

GSOLE has created an archivist position to help organize and store its content for efficient future retrieval. This is a great position for anyone who wants to apply the archival research methods they have studied. The job description is as follows:

As an organization that operates predominantly virtually, GSOLE has generated a lot of online artifacts associated with its daily operations, and the resources it provides on the Online Literacy Educator community (e.g., conference, webinars, publications, certification course). As a result, we are looking for a head archivist who will take on the following responsibilities...
  • Recruit members of Archive Committee
  • Create a proposal for organizing, displaying, retrieving and sunsetting existing GSOLE artifacts
  • Collaborate with leadership on formalized policy and procedure for the on-going creation, display, and retrieval of future artifacts
  • Work with the Content Curator on design and retrieval of artifacts from the GSOLE website
  • Make videos of presenters' sessions available during the GSOLE conference
  • Coordinate with presenters featured in videos on the long-term storage of GSOLE conference session and webinar videos
  • Attend Executive Board meetings (monthly)

If you are interested in this position or you know someone who might be interested, please have them contact me at kdepew@odu.edu.

Publish with GSOLE

Consider publishing in one of GSOLE's three Online Literacy Open Resource (OLOR) journals:

  • OLOR Effective Practices (peer-reviewed) publishes brief, practical articles sharing teaching strategies for online, hybrid, and digitally enhanced learning.
  • Research in Online Literacy Education (ROLE) (peer-reviewed) publishes original research and scholarship in literacy-based online education.
  • OLOR Reviews publishes reviews of books and technologies related to online literacy education.
